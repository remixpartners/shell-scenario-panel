Leaders are being handed a false choice.

Two stories dominate the conversation about AI and the public sector. Both are incomplete, and both come from people with a stake in your answer.

Regulate it
Task forces and risk frameworks. Necessary, but reactive. It tells you what to prevent, not what to build.
Outsource it
Pick a platform, sign the partnership, hope for the best. Convenient for the vendor. Expensive, and sticky, for you.
Another way
Decide deliberately. Keep the value local. Move first, on terms that serve your residents and your tax base.

The municipal paradox.

82%
of businesses with fewer than five employees say generative AI is “not applicable” to them.
<20
Only employers under 20 people expect AI to grow their headcount. Bigger firms expect cuts.
~50%
of a typical city’s employment is small and mid-sized business, the economic base getting the least help.

Big employers get billions in help adopting AI, and expect to cut jobs, which erodes the local tax base. The small businesses that would actually hire are left on their own. The city bears the cost and misses the upside.

Adoption and hiring-expectation figures: SBA Office of Advocacy, “AI in Business: Small Firms Closing In,” September 2025, using Census Bureau Business Trends and Outlook Survey data. Share-of-employment figure approximate.
