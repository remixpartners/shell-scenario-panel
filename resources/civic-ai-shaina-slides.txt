Physical implications: the geography of demand is being redrawn
As work and movement change, so does the demand for every kind of space — office, retail, housing, care, and logistics.
Rising needs: senior housing and physical care, logistics & delivery footprints, and lab / health space.
Falling needs: routine office space and, as AVs scale, parking structures.
The play: reposition vulnerable office and retail stock before vacancy compounds in the urban core.

DEEP DIVE · 03
Infrastructure: power is the new constraint on growth
Whether a region can host the AI economy increasingly comes down to electrons — and how fast they can be delivered.
Data centers could consume 9–17% of U.S. electricity by 2030, up from ~4.5% today — estimates are ~60% higher than two years ago (EPRI).
Site selection has flipped: delivering 300+ MW in under 36 months now outweighs fiber connectivity (CBRE).
Plan together: generation mix, siting, construction jobs, water, and the movement of people & goods.

DEEP DIVE · 04
Startups & small business: where AI becomes local jobs
Productivity gains stay local when small businesses adopt AI and new companies form and scale at home.
Adoption is inflecting: 55% of small businesses used AI in 2025, up from 39% — and 47% → 68% among 10–100-employee firms (Thryv).
The enterprise–SMB gap is closing fast — historically a full tech cycle, now roughly a year (SBA Office of Advocacy).
Two moves: seed the startup ecosystem beyond the core models, and drive Main Street adoption support.

DEEP DIVE · 05
Workforce: prepare for the tasks that remain — and the ones AI creates
The near-term squeeze is real and uneven — but technology has always created far more work than it has erased.
The pinch is at the entry level: AI-adopting firms cut junior hiring ~13% (Cornell); leaders flag heavy exposure in entry-level white-collar tasks.
History rhymes: ~60% of today's jobs are in occupations that didn't exist in 1940 (Goldman Sachs).
Strategy: reskill toward judgment, problem-framing, and human-facing roles — aligned to the local industry plan.
