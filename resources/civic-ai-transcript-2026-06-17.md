## Merged Meeting Summary
### IDEO context: interview, business decline, and governance concerns
- Justin Massa spoke with **Elle Ramey** on **2026-06-16** regarding her **IDEO** interview, aiming to be candid about IDEO’s situation.
- IDEO’s revenue was described as dropping from a peak of **$265M (2019)** to **~$60M last year**, with contributing factors cited as:
  - The “great resignation” disproportionately impacting mid-level talent.
  - Loss of client-side relationship owners, breaking continuity.
  - Inability to retain/pay senior talent.
  - Risky financial obligations driven by “inherent optimism.”
- Market perception was discussed as shifting: IDEO used to feel like a “safe” hire, but the market “fell out of love with design thinking,” and IDEO was viewed as slow to evolve it. Additional pressures mentioned: pandemic/post-pandemic economics, overstaffing, leadership instability, and high partner turnover (claim: ~80% over 5–6 years).
- Anecdotal “scuttlebutt” example: Builder’s Vision allegedly paid **$2M** and found an IDEO deliverable “unusable.”
- Ownership/PE claims: IDEO described as wholly owned by private equity; owner named as **HDY Holdings (Japan)**, connected to **QKYU**, with a list of purported affiliated acquisitions (not verified and noted as possibly out of date): Sidley, SY Partners, Upstatement, Godfrey Dadich Partners, and Digital Kitchen. The roll-up strategy was characterized as an “anti creative agency roll-up” that “has not gone well,” with claims of underinvestment and poor decisions.
- Governance anecdote: In a partner meeting about selling the remainder of the company to Q (a **$47M** deal), Justin raised concerns about unclear “true up” clauses and the absence of a “clawback provision.” He said it appeared the partner side had not engaged legal counsel; someone called Q’s CFO mid-meeting and reported there was no clawback provision shortly before the vote (with unconfirmed speculation about potential waiver language).
### Generative AI implications: design thinking, new paradigms, and “thought partner” limits
- The group debated whether there is a “next iteration of design thinking” versus an entirely new paradigm driven by GenAI; the view expressed was that AI may change innovation work so much that legacy methods don’t map cleanly (analogies: high-frequency trading vs. faster regular trading; “bit for horses” when driving cars).
- Claude (and similar models) were described as useful within a provided framework but not consistently producing high-consultant-grade, multi-angle creativity without sufficient context. Example: an op-ed converted to a government press release felt “plain,” largely because key context lived in the author’s head from many recent conversations.
- Justin emphasized that evaluating only a base model/harness understates capability; performance improves with layers such as connectors/tools, broader skills, and large volumes of institutional knowledge so the system behaves in-context (analogy: lighting gasoline doesn’t tell you a car’s gas mileage).
### Codifying consulting/innovation into AI “skills” and a knowledge “vault”
- Justin described building an AI “skill” that emulates a high-end IDEO-style engagement: plan-to-win cascade → jobs-to-be-done → design thinking → simulated sales calls → product idea exploration → synthetic research subjects → PRD, producing a product spec in ~2 hours when paired with institutional knowledge and then handing off to a coding engine.
- Example test used “Acme Corp” and “seventy synthetic coyotes” over six research rounds, yielding an unexpected insight about valuing the chase.
- A “vault” / private wiki (“Remix agent”) concept was discussed: continuously ingesting “off-gas” from conversations into a searchable library that can answer queries with citations to internal sources. Justin described a ~**430-article** private Wikipedia and claimed producing **2,000+** structured notes/articles since the start of the year.
- The system was described as trained to flag when it pulls content “beyond the vault” to distinguish internal knowledge from external inference.
### Knowledge management tooling: markdown-first, Obsidian/GitHub vs Notion/Google Docs
- The group discussed how to structure information so AI can reliably use it (clear instructions at the top, strong document structure, metadata).
- Justin advocated for simple, markdown-first sources (fewer tool calls, easier parsing, structure natively understood by models) and warned that PDFs/complex formats cost tokens and lose structure.
- Notion was described as effectively “a collection of markdown files” with a user-friendly interface, though markdown isn’t frictionless in Windows/Google ecosystems.
- Obsidian was presented as a local-first vault that can sync across devices; Justin described keeping materials in a GitHub repository across machines to enable sharing with collaborators.
- Cost comparison cited: Obsidian **~$4/person/month** vs Notion **~$20/person/month**.
- No final decision was made on a single source of truth or a migration plan from Google Drive/Docs.

Modular offerings were considered a likely learning.
- The end product remained open (consulting offering vs. nonprofit-style convening vs. op-eds vs. productized “remix” model for every state), and the “case for change” framing was acknowledged as not yet fully working.
### Chicago-focused analysis, jobs impacts, and policy response ideas
- Plans were made to consolidate workstreams and jointly edit core analysis (including [Speaker 3]’s document and Justin’s SMB analysis). Additional workforce analysis using job codes/O*NET was referenced, with open questions about shareability beyond client contexts.
- Justin recommended two podcast appearances by **Alex Imas** (UChicago economist; mentioned as Google DeepMind’s Chief AI Economist) emphasizing jobs vs. tasks, uncertainty about willingness to pay for human vs. AI outputs across domains, and the need for a “Manhattan Project of data” to understand value allocation.
- The group discussed expectations shifting away from a sudden “jobs apocalypse” toward uneven, gradual disruption (“drip drip”) that may fail to trigger political action; an example was shared of a law-firm partner planning to cut headcount without backfilling.
- Proposed city response: build ongoing monitoring systems with activation triggers, plus repeatable rapid reskilling infrastructure; coding bootcamps were discussed as either a hindsight mistake or proof that rapid mobilization is possible.
- The solution space was framed as needing to move beyond restricting AI use; examples of pro-growth civic levers included simplifying regulatory hurdles for 1–2 person businesses and exploring civic-owned compute.
- Justin proposed a **Shell-style scenario planning** exercise (AI-driven scenario panel) using prior conversation transcripts to generate Chicago scenarios (including a two-by-two) and monitoring indicators for the group to review together.
